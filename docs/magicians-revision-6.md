**Revision 6: what nobody resolved by the deadline is read, not assumed (post 18).**

@Zealynx Both halves hold, and they have one cause: the expiry recorded every missing decisive finding as `UNDETERMINED(TIMEOUT)` without asking the method. Revision 5 fixed *when* an `UNDETERMINED` may be recorded, not what the record says once the decision deadline has passed: a quorum formed inside the window was lost if nobody resolved it in time, a case without one became `METHOD_UNDETERMINED` or `TIMEOUT` depending on whether someone called between `evidenceCloseAt` and `decisionDeadline`, and with `evidenceWindow == decisionWindow` that interval did not exist. Section 15 has declarations key flows on a round's reason, so this is a defect of the specification, not of an implementation. Thanks for tracing both paths.

Revision 6 takes the first option for evidenced questions:

1. `registerPolicy` refuses a track with `evidenceWindow >= decisionWindow` (Section 3.2), so a question still pending when its window closes always has time to be resolved.
2. `expireDecision`, and `enforceDeadline` for a case still in its round, record a missing decisive finding as a resolution at the deadline would have recorded it (Section 9.1). For an evidenced question the registry calls the rule's `evaluate` once, with an empty bundle: a quorum formed inside the window is recorded as the panel's `TRUE` or `FALSE`, with the rule as acceptor, and decides the round; no quorum is recorded `UNDETERMINED` with `METHOD_UNDETERMINED`, and the burden rule applies as usual. `TIMEOUT` is left for a rule that cannot answer at all (a revert, an exhausted allowance, a malformed answer), so the expiry always completes. An unresolved premise still gives `TIMEOUT`, a premise with another value `PREMISE_NOT_ESTABLISHED`.
3. The read cannot be steered by whoever calls it. It carries a fixed gas allowance (at least 1,000,000) and the expiry reverts unless the whole allowance can be forwarded, so nobody can starve the rule into the failure that would turn its answer into `TIMEOUT`; only the first three words of the answer are copied; and a resolution or an expiry during which the rule called back into the registry and changed the case reverts (under the optimistic track the silence rule is open to anyone after its deadline, so a rule could otherwise apply it in the middle of the read).

For formal questions the second option applies, because the registry cannot do the same: it holds only the hash of the pinned inputs, not the inputs, so it cannot run the verifier at expiry. A judged question is in the same position, since its method pushes a Final result of its source. There `TIMEOUT` keeps a narrower meaning, which Section 15 now states for a declaration keyed on reasons: the inputs, or the source's result, were not brought in time; `METHOD_UNDETERMINED` says the method ran and reached no answer, and for an evidenced question it no longer depends on whether anyone resolved before the deadline. @kurosato, this is the piece the per-path declarations of post 16 were missing: the per-path wording of Section 15 is already in the merged Draft, and a flow keyed on a reason can now rely on what the reason means.

A review round cut by the hard deadline can still have its evidence window close at its decision deadline, since both are capped; the read covers that case too. Function signatures and ERC-165 identifiers do not change; three deployable units do (the policy registry, the resolution module and the decision module). The suite grows from 105 to 125 tests, twenty of them on the expiry: the quorum nobody resolved and the case without one, each compared field by field with the resolution that could have been made, every way a rule can fail to answer, the expiry refused with too little gas, a sweep of gas limits that never yields `TIMEOUT` from a rule needing 900,000 gas, a 300,000-byte answer, the call-back cases, premises, formal and judged questions, `enforceDeadline` and the truncated review round.
