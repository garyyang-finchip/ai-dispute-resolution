# Revision 4 note (reply for the Magicians thread)

*Posted as a reply on topic 30121 after the first two reviews (posts 4 and 5) and the two answers to them (posts 6 and 7). Plain text for the forum; the links are the companion repository and the pull request.*

---

**Revision 4 — the source states the breaches; exclusivity on the contested point; settlements held until the attested deadline.**

Revision 4 is the one announced in the two replies above. It answers the findings of Carlos (Zealynx, post 4) and Mikhail (Revinand, post 5), which were both right and both wider than their first example, and it changes the admission rule, the exclusivity key and the obligations of a source. The pull request carries the text; the repository carries the contracts, tests, vectors and examples.

**The source states the breaches (post 5).** The filer used to choose both the decisive sub-question and its direction, and admission checked neither against anything: a question that holds whatever the facts, sustained when `TRUE`, or the direction in which the respondent's good performance is the breach, passed the five criteria, was verified correctly, sustained and enacted — a refund for having been paid once. `IADRFObligationSource` gains `recognizesBreach(object, eventRef, obligationKind, proposition, breachWhen)`: the source states which proposition, taking which value, is a breach of which obligation under the norm it attested for the event. Criterion 3 of admission ("norm pre-declared and breach recognized") requires it for the decisive sub-question with the claim's `supportsWhen`; a claim the source does not recognize is refused with `NormNotApplicable`. Recognition is a statement about the norm, not about the facts — a source may recognize a proposition that only evidence or judgment can settle — and it pre-judges nothing; premises are not checked, because only the decisive finding is read against the direction. The effects were already constrained by the binding (authorized effects, `SUBSTANTIVE` category); the finding that makes an effect available now is too. The interface identifier of `IADRFObligationSource` is therefore new (`0xa52f2870`); those of the two registries are unchanged.

**Exclusivity on the contested point (posts 4 and 5).** The key moves from the claim's headline `proposition` to the decisive sub-question's proposition: `keccak256(abi.encode(source, info.obligationRef, decisive.proposition))`. Two descriptions of one point now share a key (the headline variation is refused as `Duplicate`), and two breaches of one obligation do not, because the source recognizes one proposition per breach. For the limit obligation of the example that is what makes a further breach actionable: the escrow attests each settlement beyond the first as an event of its own (`settlementEventRef(n)`, with its own instant and adoption deadline) and recognizes the duplicate-settlement verifier's proposition for that ordinal (statement `adrf:prop/duplicate-settlement/v2`, one proposition per `(ledger, call, ordinal)`), so a third charge after the ruling on the second is a new case with its own refund, while the second cannot be tried twice. The verifiers now publish their canonical propositions through small statement libraries, which is how a source recognizes exactly them.

**Held until the attested deadline (post 4).** A source that attests an adoption deadline MUST keep what a ruling on that event could affect enactable by its executors until that deadline (Section 5.1); the clock check guaranteed the time, this guarantees the value. The example escrow no longer credits the provider at settlement: every settlement is held until `settledAt + disputePeriod`, anyone may `release` it afterwards, `withdrawBalance` takes released funds only, and a refund takes the held settlement the case was about (a sustained delivery claim takes the earliest held charge). An executor that finds the value gone reports `Failed` with the reason (`keccak256("settlement released before enactment")`), the ruling stands, and Section 10.3 says whose failure that is.

**What else is in the text.** The source's duties (derive every attestation from its own state; recognize one proposition per breach), the Rationale paragraphs on direction, on the key following the point rather than the headline, and on held value, the Security Considerations on direction and choice of question and on enactability, and the test-case items for admission, exclusivity and the new recognition and held-value scenarios. The claim grammar, the methods, the state machine, the ruling and the J-profile are unchanged.

**Tests and sizes.** The suite is 79 tests (nine new in `ADRFRecognition.t.sol`: the inverted direction, a trivially true decisive question, an unrecognized proposition and a wrong kind, recognition as the source's own statement through a mock source, exclusivity on the decisive proposition, each settlement beyond the first as its own case with its own refund, settlements held until the attested deadline, a late executor reporting `Failed`, a delivery claim refunding the held charge), and the existing flows and vectors were adapted (the duplicate-charge case names settlement 2 as its event and the verifier's proposition for it). Four deployable units changed bytecode (`ADRFBindingModule`, which checks the source interface identifier; `ADRFFilingModule`, now 8,784 bytes; `DuplicateSettlementVerifier`; `ADRFServiceEscrow`, now 9,209 bytes with its settlement ledger); all stay within the 16,777,216-gas cap at the schedule the revision-3 run measured (the escrow at about 88% of it). The seven-phase evidence script was rehearsed again on Anvil with the Sepolia flags; both refunds were enacted from held settlements.

**Sepolia.** The revision-3 deployment and its record stay as they are (`docs/sepolia-evidence.md`, `deployments/sepolia.json`). The revision-4 contracts are deployed in a second run, recorded separately (`deployments/sepolia-rev4.json`, `docs/sepolia-evidence-rev4.md`); its note follows here.
