**Revision 5 — evidence still pending is not a final UNDETERMINED; a delivery is judged against the terms and the procedure in force when it was made.**

Revision 5 carries the two changes promised in post 11 and in the reply to post 13. The pull request carries the text; the repository carries the contracts, the tests and the evidence script. Thanks to Mikhail (Revinand) for the precise path to the first, and to Ted (ted0x) for two pull requests, both now merged into the repository: a reproduction of that path with the registry-side fix and seven cases (ai-dispute-resolution#2), and the seven tests behind the second (ai-dispute-resolution#1).

**Pending versus terminal (post 13).** Resolution is permissionless and a finding is recorded once, so the first caller fixes it; with a method that returns the same `UNDETERMINED` for "not yet" and for "never", an unrelated account could close an evidenced question before the panel had spoken. The registry now separates the two itself, by the rule proposed in #2: an `UNDETERMINED` from an evidence rule, and in this revision from a verifier as well, is recorded only after the round's `evidenceCloseAt`; until then, that instant included, `resolveEvidenced` and `resolveFormal` revert with `WindowOpen("evidence")`, record nothing and leave the sub-question open. `TRUE` or `FALSE` is recorded when it comes, which Section 8.1 makes safe by requiring a method's determinate answer to be final for its round: the half that a registry cannot enforce, as #2 pointed out. The reference `AttestorQuorumRule` meets that duty: it is built for one dispute registry, takes only strict-majority panels of distinct attestors (2k > n), counts attestations per round and only inside the case's evidence window, and closes a round once a quorum forms, so the finding is the first quorum the panel forms inside the window, or `UNDETERMINED` once the window has closed without one, whoever calls and whenever. Judged findings are unaffected: the judgment method pushes only a Final result of its source. A distinct "pending" value was considered and not adopted: a value that must never be stored would sit in the finding type as a trap, while a reverted call records nothing by construction.

**The terms and the procedure in force at the event (posts 10 to 12).** The invariant Ted's suite pins down is now in the specification. Under the obligation sources, a source attests each event as it stood — the norm in force and when it was declared, the event instant, the adoption deadline — and does not rewrite those attestations when its terms change: amended terms reach only later events. The registry enforces the procedure half, which it can see: a `PRE_COMMIT` binding governs exactly the events that occur while it is in force, from its registration until its revocation or `validUntil`, and keeps governing them afterwards. A binding registered after an event does not reach back to it (`BindingNotInForce`; a `POST_DISPUTE` binding still can, with the respondent's acceptance), a new policy version therefore cannot lengthen or shorten an earlier delivery's filing window, and a revocation no longer cuts off a dispute about an event that occurred while the binding was in force — before this revision it stopped every unfiled claim, which would have let a source hold the value Section 5.1 requires and still defeat the claim by revoking first. The binding record keeps the revocation instant. The reference escrow enacts rulings made under any binding it registered, so moving to amended terms orphans no earlier case.

**Tests and toolchain.** The suite is 105 tests: Ted's fourteen — the seven amended-terms cases (`test/ADRFNormRevisions.t.sol`) unchanged, and the seven pending-evidence cases (`test/ADRFPendingEvidence.t.sol`), whose filing helper now makes its delivery under the binding it registers, since a binding no longer reaches back — seven on final answers (a late attestation refused, finality of a quorum, revision before a quorum, panel rules, binding of attestations, a verifier's early `UNDETERMINED`, a review round's own tally), four for the procedure in force (an earlier delivery under the revoked binding with its own filing window, the amended window not reaching it, a later delivery under the amended procedure and terms, enactment after the escrow moved on), one more admission case for the binding period (no reaching back; `validUntil` ends it), and the existing cases adapted — the revocation case now checks that a revocation reaches only later events. Ted also found that `forge test` failed in every `setUp` under Foundry 1.7 unless the code size limit was raised on the command line; `foundry.toml` now raises it for test and script contracts, and the suite runs as is under 1.5 and 1.7. No interface identifier changes; five deployable units have new bytecode (`ADRFBindingModule`, `ADRFFilingModule`, `ADRFResolutionModule`, `AttestorQuorumRule`, `ADRFServiceEscrow`), all within Sepolia's per-transaction cap.

**Sepolia.** The third run adds both scenarios to the existing flows: a delivery under the first binding, the escrow moving to an amended policy, a later delivery under amended terms, and one evidenced case on each — a quorum inside the window on the earlier one, ruled and refunded under the revoked binding, a single attestation pending and then a final `UNDETERMINED` on the later one — with the refusals checked against the deployed contracts. It runs on the revision as published here, and its note will follow in this thread.
